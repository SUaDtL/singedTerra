# Battle console integrated visual system

**Governs:** the battle-console visual and semantic surfaces under `client/src/ui/`, their scoped styles and browser tests, deterministic console assets under `client/public/art/battle-console-integrated/`, their build/contract tooling under `scripts/assets/` and `.codearbiter/contracts/battle-console/`, and the accepted superseding decision `.codearbiter/decisions/0018-preact-battle-console-semantic-ownership.md`.

## Status and gate

This revision remains **proposed**. Revision 9 earned a clean exact-hash specialist review and explicit maintainer approval, but the subsequent implementation-aware architecture challenge rejected its assumption that the current imperative DOM ownership model was itself worth preserving. Revision 10 selected deliberate Preact ownership replacement but its exact-hash review found eleven unique contract defects. Revision 11 closed those defects; the maintainer-bounded final broad review then found seven concrete blockers and zero discretionary suggestions. Revision 12 closed the global-command inventory, asynchronous lifecycle races, accepted-and-hashed ADR authority, review aggregate self-cycle, mutable campaign preservation, physical-device provenance, and actual TypeScript executable graph. Revision 13 introduced an OpenSSH detached-signature fallback after F-01 proved that the host-history API cannot provide cryptographic item identity. Revision 14 removed that invented key/signature requirement and restored direct exact-hash maintainer approval. Approved-plan execution then produced concrete F-02/F-03 evidence: authenticated GitHub CI has no challenge-dispatch surface and no callable non-controller signed physical-device authority exists. Revision 15 applies the maintainer's explicit correction by retaining those capabilities as final-delivery gates while removing them from the local implementation critical path. Approval of this specification authorizes only its dependency-ordered plan. Product implementation cannot begin until Gate 0 captures the worktree and semantic baselines and Gate 1 locks every candidate-independent local contract in an explicitly maintainer-approved preflight decision. ## Architecture

- Sharp 0.35.3 builds deterministic, state-free raster chrome at development time.
- PixiJS 8.20.0 paints non-interactive ready-state chrome in one canvas below semantic DOM ink.
- Preact 10.29.8 owns exactly one in-scope semantic presentation tree, including its keyed portals. Its live DOM nodes remain the only owners of visible text, accessibility, focus, dialogs, and element-local pointer/keyboard/touch events; Pixi never owns semantics or input. A locked input arbiter assigns every global and local key path to exactly one owner and prevents Preact/native activation from duplicating the existing window-capture gameplay path.
- Business behavior remains outside the presentation tree. One callback-free `BattleConsolePresentationState` enters the tree and one discriminated `BattleConsoleIntent` union leaves it for the existing controller/domain boundary.
- The legacy in-scope console builders, cached-node graph, embedded CSS, imperative sync writers, listener closures, and selector-as-application-API paths are retired rather than mounted beside Preact.
- One typed `1388x212` logical topology positions Pixi chrome and semantic DOM in loading, ready, fallback, and context-loss presentation.
- Canvas 2D remains the battlefield/gameplay-world renderer. Game state, deterministic physics, networking, dialogs, routes, and static hosting do not move into Pixi.

`1388x212` is the canonical logical and raster surface. The old `1388x198` underlay is not a candidate: displaying it at 212 pixels high creates roughly 7.1% vertical stretch. Non-uniform scaling is forbidden, and the old asset is handled only by the retirement manifest.

### Implementation-aware ownership challenge

The implementation-aware audit found `client/src/ui/HUD.ts` at 13,666 lines with roughly 9,200 lines of injected CSS, at least 234 element creations, 57 listener registrations with no paired removals in the class, and a large cached `HTMLElement` graph. Its `destroy()` releases only the Pixi controller, and production has no call that destroys the persistent HUD on battle exit. Seventeen focused HUD tests contain hundreds of `querySelector` calls and no role/text queries. The view also performs manual semantic reparenting, focus recovery, DOM-derived active state, and multi-root class/style mutation. Gate 0's content-addressed baseline, not these explanatory counts, is authoritative if the dirty worktree changes.

The mitigating fact is architectural: `main.ts` already communicates with `HUD` through state setters and semantic callbacks rather than receiving private element references. That seam makes a deliberate ownership replacement safer than hardening the monolith in place. Three end states were compared. Preserving and hardening vanilla DOM retains most of the debt; extracting an imperative controller improves state flow but retains manual reconciliation; one stable Preact tree removes the in-scope builder/listener/cache ownership while preserving semantic HTML and the existing gameplay boundary. The chosen end state is therefore Preact as the sole in-scope semantic owner, never a second tree beside the legacy console.

Exact `preact@10.29.8` is the approved runtime candidate: MIT, no required runtime dependencies, npm SLSA provenance, and no OSV vulnerability record for that exact version at review time. `preact@10.27.2` is explicitly rejected because it falls inside the affected range of GHSA-36hm-qxxp-pg3m/CVE-2026-22028. No install occurs until the dependency task reaches its governed execution point; the exact lockfile, notice, audit, signature/provenance, bundle, CSP, and static-host checks remain blocking.

The maintainer also authorizes a scoped no-cost modernization during this rewrite. Exact planned direct pins are `lucide@1.33.0`, `vite@8.2.2`, `vitest@4.1.11`, `@vitest/coverage-v8@4.1.11`, `tsx@4.23.12`, and `concurrently@10.0.5`; existing latest-compatible pins remain `@playwright/test@1.62.1`, `jsdom@30.0.1`, `@types/node@24.13.3`, `pixi.js@8.20.0`, `sharp@0.35.3`, and the actual TypeScript 7.0.2 compiler. Package ranges are replaced by those exact direct pins in the governed dependency task. Live npm metadata shows permissive licenses and SLSA attestations for the six upgrades, and an exact-version OSV batch plus the current full npm audit returned no vulnerability records. `@supabase/supabase-js@2.112.3` and Supabase CLI `2.115.0` are deliberately excluded because they alter unrelated network/backend contracts; `@types/node@26.2.0` is excluded because the governed runtime is Node 24. This authority does not permit pre-Gate installation or an unreviewed transitive.

### Ownership replacement scope

The Preact boundary contains exactly the closed semantic registry: the integrated Commander/Ballistics/Fire Control console, Weapon and Armory disclosure/dialog behavior, movement/Fuel, Angle/Power/Wind, Settings, Fire, and first-salvo coach states. Discovery covers an explicit root set rather than one ancestry chain: the command-console host; every in-scope sibling, portal, drawer, and reparented modal host in closed/open/closing states; and each in-scope or out-of-scope trigger that enters those surfaces, including pause-origin Settings. Match ledger/player rows, connection/turn-watch/quick-chat surfaces, round-over/victory/progression overlays, lobby/account routes, and gameplay-world canvas remain outside this replacement, but their cross-owner entry and focus-return edges into an in-scope surface remain contract inputs. The existing `HUD` public façade may temporarily remain as a compatibility controller for those out-of-scope surfaces, but it may hold no in-scope raw element, listener, style, focus, or callback owner after retirement. All communication across that seam is typed presentation state and `BattleConsoleIntent` only.

## Gate order and authority

1. **Gate 0 — attest the exact pre-mutation product.** The controller finalizes `preservation/baseline-content.json`, materializes those dirty bytes into a content-addressed read-only snapshot, builds and launches one fresh no-HMR baseline server, records served-byte closure and a baseline nonce, then captures `topology/semantic-baseline.json` and callback probes only from that origin. A write-once controller receipt publishes the baseline content, snapshot, server, and semantic hashes after those artifacts exist. The receipt proves ordering and hash consistency, not external authorship. No baseline artifact references its later receipt/index. The existing protected preview is preserved but forbidden as Gate-0 evidence.
2. **Gate 1 — lock candidate-independent contracts and verifiers.** Durable source, reference registration, visual oracle, mutation corpus, root-set semantic/chrome registries, presentation architecture, input arbiter, intent-transition traces, production lifecycle triggers, style scope, projections, dynamic appearance, browser/CDP fixtures, exact toolchain/dependency graph, performance budgets, responsive design goldens, ownership, retirement, preservation rules, comparator/generator/extractor code, evidence suites, and local secondary-audit configuration are complete. Before product mutation, the locked verifier/browser replays the full mechanical semantic inventory/lifecycle/intent probes against a fresh server from the Gate-0 snapshot; it must exactly match the externally published semantic baseline. Gate 1 also locks the old-owner-to-new-owner semantic key map and proves the candidate cannot mount legacy and Preact console owners concurrently. Delivery-only terminal-runner and signed-physical-device schemas may be locked here, but provider availability and execution are not Gate-1 prerequisites.
3. **Gate 1 evidence and review.** `contract-core-index.json` hashes only the candidate-independent contracts and verifier inputs, including the direct-approval policy, accepted superseding ADR/decision receipt, and closed expected-review-unit manifest. AC-01 through AC-07 produce immutable reports against that hash; `preflight-evidence-index.json` hashes those reports. The chain is fixed as locked expected manifest → independently authored unit reports → immutable unit index → separately authored triage → separately authored aggregate `review/strong-adversarial.json` → exact review-set receipt. Every unit binds a distinct reviewer lane/task identifier, author label, and exact raw report/transcript-snapshot hash. The aggregate hashes the expected manifest, every unit, the unit index, triage, core index, and evidence index; the receipt binds those same hashes and the exact reviewer packet shown to the maintainer without claiming provider-attested authorship. The downstream `preflight-envelope.json` hashes the aggregate and receipt; no artifact hashes itself.
4. **Gate 1 direct approval and seal.** The maintainer directly approves the exact original-resolution review packet and its SHA-256 in the active task. The controller stores the exact verbatim approval text and available task identity in a write-once decision receipt. A local read-only verifier checks artifact hashes, display hashes, receipt schema, exact quoted approval, chronology, and campaign checkpoint consistency; it explicitly does not claim cryptographic authorship or provider immutability. `preflight-envelope.json` hashes the core index, evidence index, strong-review record, review-set receipt, and decision receipt. None references the envelope or is indexed by an object it references. Any contract, verifier, evidence, review, decision, or receipt change invalidates the envelope and returns to Gate 1.
5. **Gate 2 — candidate implementation and immutable execution.** Product code/assets may change inside the mutation allowlist, test-first, against the preflight envelope. The frozen candidate/build and Gate-1 verifier/toolchain become separate content-addressed read-only snapshots. Every suite executes fresh from those snapshots with module/executable read tracing; mutable worktree, preload hooks, alternate loaders, dev/HMR servers, and writes to verifier/product mounts are unavailable. A fresh attested production static server launches from the indexed build after freeze. `final/evidence-index.json` hashes all pre-decision reports for the same snapshots and server.
6. **Gate 3 — closure, visual acceptance, and independently recomputed local terminal audit.** AC-43 emits its own locked-snapshot execution record. The packet references product, evidence, and closure; the direct maintainer approval receipt references the exact packet and hashes; AC-39 emits `final/decision-verification.json` with the complete receipt-consistency trace. The six-input envelope hashes them. A fresh local secondary process launched from the read-only verifier snapshot receives a new challenge, re-runs AC-43 from raw product/evidence/snapshot/server inputs, independently re-runs AC-39 receipt/packet consistency, compares both outcomes byte-for-byte with the local records, then validates the envelope. The process has a separate PID, clean environment, explicit read-only mounts and complete read trace; it proves repeatability and artifact integrity without claiming external provenance or maintainer authorship.
7. **Final-delivery gates — external terminal provenance and signed physical target.** Only after the maintainer separately authorizes delivery, F-02 requires a challenge-bound externally proven runner result and F-03 requires a non-controller signed physical compact-target result. Their preserved feasibility receipts remain BLOCKED until suitable providers exist. They are leaf gates for delivery readiness: no Gate 0/1/2/3 task, local completion claim, or visual-acceptance decision depends on them. They must pass before any later delivery claim that explicitly includes external-runner or physical-device proof; they may never be fabricated, substituted with controller prose, or satisfied by desktop emulation.

Workers and tests may validate approval receipts; they may never create, infer, upgrade, copy forward, or choose approval. `authority/approval-policy.json` freezes `direct-maintainer-approval-v1`: a fresh explicit maintainer message approving an exact artifact SHA-256 or displayed packet is the governance authority. The controller may transcribe that message into a receipt but may not invent or broaden it. Because F-01 proved the host API lacks provider-declared item-bound integrity identities, every receipt must set `cryptographicAuthorshipClaim: false` and `providerImmutabilityClaim: false`. Those limitations are honest evidence boundaries, not blockers and not permission for a worker-generated approval.

Every approval receipt uses locked `canonical-json/v1`: recursively sorted object keys, arrays in declared order, UTF-8, LF, and no BOM or insignificant whitespace. The two claim fields must be `false`. Decision-specific schemas restrict purpose, decision, scope, required artifact/display hashes, and rejection history for ADR acceptance, registration, responsive goldens, review-set receipt, Gate-1 approval, and final visual approval. A missing, ambiguous, stale, broadened, or worker-authored approval remains blocking.

## Lifecycle and fallback resources

The state machine is:

`unmounted -> loading -> ready|fallback|destroyed`

`ready -> fallback|destroyed`

`fallback -> destroyed`

`destroyed` is terminal. There is no in-place GPU recovery. A later route/controller remount may start a new lifecycle; the failed controller cannot return to ready. Asset/init/decode/update/render/resize failure and context loss atomically enter fallback. The Preact root mounts once per lifecycle; Pixi state changes update props/state and may not remount the semantic tree. Every production battle exit, restart, rematch, quit, route/account transition, and page teardown in `lifecycle-triggers.json` must execute its ordered sequence; direct destroy tests alone are insufficient. Failed create/restore, rejected promises, overlapping starts, rematch/quit during load, superseded generations, and destroy during lazy import must cancel or reject stale completion before any root/controller/Pixi resource can mount. Restart is specifically destroy → proven zero → one generation-winning mount.

Loading, ready, context-loss transition, and fallback keep identical keyed semantic node objects and typed rectangles; only material ownership changes. Fallback retains exactly the presentation root, typed intent bridge, controller projection observer, media/DPR/visual-viewport listeners, and semantic input handlers required for responsive DOM operation. It has zero Pixi application, adapter, canvas, texture, display object, GPU/context handler, render callback, or asset-load resources. Destroy unmounts Preact and releases presentation effects/timers/refs, controller resources, and Pixi resources to zero. Static and runtime gates reject coordinate-bearing legacy console geometry, a second semantic root, or a surviving retired listener/style owner.

Forced context loss, resize/DPR/orientation while fallback, destroy-during-load/import, repeated destroy, create/restore rejection, overlapping/superseded starts, rematch/quit during load, and hot-seat plus network battle→lobby→battle journeys must prove state-specific resource cardinalities and responsive same-topology semantics. Between sessions there are zero old roots/portals/effects/listeners/intent bridges/Pixi resources, no stale focus/state/intent, every stale completion is rejected, and exactly one winning generation owns one fresh lifecycle even though module bytes remain cached.

## Delivery state distinctions

Automated green means locally verified only. Gate-3 approval means visually accepted only for the exact bound worktree. Neither means staged, committed, pushed, PR-opened, merged, released, deployed, or production-proven. Each remains separately governed and requires explicit maintainer direction. F-02 and F-03 are delivery-only leaves: they do not block local green or Gate-3 visual acceptance, but any future delivery claim that includes external terminal provenance or signed physical-device proof must execute them after explicit delivery authority and must remain blocked until their genuine evidence exists.

## Open questions

None are hidden. Registration, responsive design goldens, numeric oracle thresholds, performance budgets, and browser/device fixtures are deliberate candidate-independent Gate-1 outputs with direct approval. Their absence keeps product implementation closed; it does not authorize an implementation-time guess.

## Intent completeness result

If every criterion passes and nothing else changes, the complete reference console has one declarative semantic owner, one inert Pixi chrome owner, typed state/intents, retired legacy console ownership, preserved gameplay/routes/out-of-scope HUD behavior, immutable evidence, and direct visual authority. Nothing else in the integrated-console scope remains broken. Staging, commit, PR, merge, release, deployment, and production proof remain deliberately separate governance states.
