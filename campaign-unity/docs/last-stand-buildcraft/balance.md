# Buildcraft balance evidence

The 500-wave comparison passes the approved numerical criterion: all seven single fittings and all 21 pairs were available from one genuinely earned profile, six pair choices appear on the measured frontier, and no pair dominates every other pair across all six tiers. No production balance tuning was needed after the first measurement.

This is source-bound execution of the production combat, save, purchase and research owners. It is not a rendered play-feel review, elapsed endurance session, native-device qualification, or hosted-CI result.

## Earned investment and comparison policy

Funding began from an empty profile and processed actual sequential cohorts and transactions. The final seed earned **515,420 Salvage**: 223,570 spent on permanent upgrades, 75,192 on research, and 216,658 unspent. Both equipment bays, every fitting and all six tiers were earned. Paid research reached 5× after 42.90 simulated calendar days, including 540 additional closed hours after combat funding; closed time generated no income. The acquired seed, paid research journal and completion records remain in the reports.

Every comparison forks those identical saved bytes (`10420320d79a73248d314303d4f680f1d0f2ce9b46a219108f72cf6c2dbf11dc`). It allocates half the remaining wallet to common permanent tracks and half to the selected auxiliary tracks, retaining exact debit and residue accounting. Common effects apply once across both bays. Runs use paid 5× speed and at most one bulk purchase per 15 active minutes, stop at wave 500 or defeat, and have a one-million-tick bound. All 168 comparisons executed; none substituted a gifted unlock or unavailable result.

Frontier metrics are completed wave, final hull, effective damage per simulation second, and repeatable Salvage per active hour. First-clear awards are excluded from farming rates. The Python reader independently recomputes the frontier and checks seed identity, paid allocations, income, unique kills, damage totals and purchase cadence.

These observations concern this earned budget, policy and horizon; they do not establish universal rankings for every stage of progression.

## Sensitivity and farming

The separate sensitivity report runs 18 predeclared pair/tier combinations under five variants: baseline, ±5% current-edition prices, and ±5% current-edition weapon damage. All 90 runs retain the same earned seed. The counterfactuals use disposable catalog copies, preserve all four retained editions, retain level-zero damage floors, and round integer prices to the nearest unit. Earlier acquisition is not re-simulated under those hypothetical parameters. Against baseline, observed completed-wave changes range from 0 to +40 for cheaper prices, −10 to +20 for dearer prices, −18 to 0 for lower damage, and 0 to +49 for higher damage. Those discrete outcome changes remain visible in the raw data.

The deeper farming comparison uses the same earned build (Launcher + Repair) and the same one-bulk-purchase-per-15-minutes policy. It compares requested wave-2,000 lower-tier runs with next-tier resets at waves 30 and 100. T1–T3 reach wave 2,000; T4 ends in defeat at 1,418 and T5 at 1,174. The rates below include their actual outcomes, including defeats.

The low-touch T1 run reaches wave **5,000 in 10.4992 projected active hours at 5×**, with exactly **41 accepted bulk purchases / 41 opportunities**. This meets the 10–12-hour numerical pacing target under the stated policy. The main report also retains a separate eager `afterKill` reference: 10.4563 projected hours and 2,630 purchases. Neither number represents a ten-hour elapsed play session.

## Retained failed window and regression evidence

The first complete 150-wave comparison is preserved. It **fails** the three-choice frontier criterion: only Launcher + Arc and Launcher + Mortar appear. Every pair finished that short window at full hull with this mature profile, so it did not exercise defensive choices. Its report and exact source archive remain separate from the longer passing measurement. The earlier interrupted capture also remains as a progress log: its failure exposed a measurement-observer bug in attributing Railgun armor bypass when a finite enemy shield absorbed damage. A real failing regression preceded the observer fix; the model's damage rules did not change for that repair.

Foundation regressions additionally cover the four frozen rule/content/economy tuples, retained checkpoint migration, immutable two-bay snapshots, atomic preset application, malformed/unfunded combat snapshots, exactly-once grants, and retained-run unlock receipts. Combat checks cover all 28 builds, distinct Arc hops, finite support budgets, Commander cadence, shield capacity/recovery conservation, research projections, and pause/5× equivalence. Current six-tier/seven-fitting contracts retain explicit historical four-tier/five-fitting assertions.
